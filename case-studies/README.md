# Case studies

Short write-ups of what happened when CLP was applied to real work, and what
the specification got wrong.

Entries are one of three kinds, and each entry says which it is at the top.

An **introduction** says what the project is, why it exists, and who decided.
There is one, numbered 000 because it is the entry point rather than the
newest entry.

A **record** documents one episode. It states what was attempted, what failed,
what changed in the specification as a result, and the commits that carry the
change. Records are not marketing. A record that only reports a success is not
worth writing, because the value is in the defect the episode exposed.

An **argument** makes a claim and shows the evidence behind it. It names the
protocols it was written under, states what its evidence does and does not
support, and gives the competing explanations. An argument is held to the same
standard as a record: if it cannot mark its own limits, it is not ready.

Where an entry contains text written for an outside audience, that text is
marked and is ready to use without edits.
